\begin{abstract}
Classical South Indian \emph{Ma\d{n}iprav\={a}\d{l}a} (``ruby-and-coral'') philosophical literature presents a uniquely severe challenge for Optical Character Recognition (OCR): Sanskrit and Tamil are interleaved not only across sentences and phrases, but \textbf{intra-word}, where Sanskrit nominal or verbal stems printed in the Devan\={a}gar\={\i} abugida are directly inflected with Tamil case markers, clitics, or verbal suffixes printed in the Tamil script without intervening whitespace. Furthermore, traditional multi-commentary (\emph{vy\={a}khy\={a}na-pa\~{n}caka}) editions typeset up to six distinct textual layers---root text (\emph{m\={u}lam}) and five classical commentaries---on every printed page separated by horizontal rules.

Through a systematic empirical investigation across \textbf{2,970 printed pages} ($6.67\times 10^6$ Unicode characters) of Ved\={a}nta De\'{s}ika's \emph{\'{S}r\={\i}mad-Rahasya-Traya-S\={a}ra} and its commentaries, we identify the \textbf{Granularity--Context Paradox} of biscriptal OCR: (1) whole-line and whole-page commercial/open-source OCR engines fail when presented with unsplit biscriptal lines (PaddleOCR PP-OCRv4: \textbf{0.710 CER}; Google Drive Document OCR: \textbf{0.435 CER}; bilingual Tesseract \texttt{tam+san}: \textbf{0.352 CER}), because their internal line-level language models lock onto the dominant script of each line and transliterate or garble the minority script; (2) custom dual-script CTC recognizers trained from scratch suffer from capacity and lexicon starvation (a 4.1M-parameter Kraken VGSL word recognizer trained on \textbf{92,976 real word crops} reaches \textbf{90.34\% character accuracy} / \textbf{80.53\% word accuracy}, leaving $\sim 1$ in 5 words wrong and emitting \textbf{1,456 spurious intra-word script chimeras}); and (3) wordwise script separation starves document OCR of horizontal baseline and linguistic context, causing Google Drive OCR to drop \textbf{76\%--100\% of short Sanskrit stems} and misclassify short Tamil suffixes as ASCII/Latin lookalikes due to competition from row-index tags (\texttt{\#T01}).

We resolve this paradox with a \textbf{Phrase-Strip Script-Separated Faux-Page Pipeline} (\texttt{tadevaOCR}) that combines: (i) recursive pitch-based line segmentation and mid-band ink-filtered word segmentation; (ii) a \textbf{48$\times$48 sliding-patch residual CNN} (\texttt{script\_cls.pt}, \textbf{0.9921 real-scan accuracy}) paired with minimum-ink column snapping ($\le 6$ dark pixels) to sever 1--3~px Devan\={a}gar\={\i} \emph{Shiro-rekh\={a}} overhangs at intra-word \texttt{dev} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{tam} junctions while tracking \texttt{pre\_space} flags; (iii) \textbf{same-script phrase-strip coalescing}, preserving multi-word horizontal context; and (iv) \textbf{context-engineered faux pages} featuring a $1:2$ horizontal-to-vertical proximity ratio ($28\text{ px}$ vs.\ $54\text{ px}$) and \textbf{6-consonant phonotactic script anchors} appended to narrow rows to override ASCII tag bias. On a controlled benchmark of camera-photographed \emph{S\={a}rabodhin\={\i}} blocks, \texttt{tadevaOCR} reduces raw CER to \textbf{0.112} ($3.9\times$ lower than unsplit cloud OCR and $2.1\times$ lower than custom dual-script CTC), while on flat 300-DPI scans of the five-commentary \emph{\'{S}r\={\i}mad-Rahasya-Traya-S\={a}ra} audited against diplomatic ground truth (\texttt{p00029}, 2,374 non-space characters), it achieves a \textbf{true diplomatic CER of \texttt{0.0067} (0.67\% CER / 99.33\% character accuracy; 0.80\% with whitespace)}, \textbf{100.0\% (\texttt{568/568}) tag recovery}, and a \textbf{94.6\% reduction} in stray Latin hallucinations, enabling the deterministic dissection of \textbf{11,683 commentary blocks} across \textbf{2,103 chapter pages} into \textbf{165 structured per-commentary, per-chapter files} with \textbf{zero unresolved blocks}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In the \'{S}r\={\i}vai\d{s}\d{n}ava scholastic tradition of South India (12th--19th centuries CE), philosophical treatises and commentaries were composed in \emph{Ma\d{n}iprav\={a}\d{l}a} (literally ``ruby [\emph{ma\d{n}i}, Sanskrit] and coral [\emph{prav\={a}\d{l}a}, Tamil]''), a literary register that weaves Sanskrit technical terminology and quotations seamlessly into Tamil syntactic and morphological frames. When printed in modern 20th-century scholarly editions, \emph{Ma\d{n}iprav\={a}\d{l}a} is rendered \textbf{biscriptally}: Sanskrit lexical stems, compounds, and quotations are typeset in the \textbf{Devan\={a}gar\={\i}} script, while Tamil stems, case endings, postpositions, and finite verbal morphology are typeset in the \textbf{Tamil} script.

Crucially, unlike bilingual documents where script switching occurs at paragraph, sentence, or clause boundaries, printed \emph{Ma\d{n}iprav\={a}\d{l}a} exhibits pervasive \textbf{intra-word script switching} (macaronic morphology without whitespace):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sanskrit nominal stem + Tamil case suffix / clitic:} e.g., \emph{puru\d{s}\={a}rtha-l\={a}bha-ttil} (Devan\={a}gar\={\i} stem + Tamil locative \emph{-ttil}), \emph{svar\={u}pa-pr\={a}pta-m\={a}\d{n}a} (Devan\={a}gar\={\i} stem + Tamil adjectival suffix \emph{-m\={a}\d{n}a}).
    \item \textbf{Sanskrit verbal stem + Tamil finite/non-finite inflection:} e.g., \emph{anuvadi-tt\={a}r-i\d{r}\={e}?}, \emph{sam\={a}\'{s}rayi-kkaiyil}.
    \item \textbf{Tri-segment words (\texttt{tam} $\to$ \texttt{dev} $\to$ \texttt{tam}):} Tamil demonstrative prefix \emph{ivv-} + Sanskrit stem \emph{adhik\={a}r\={a}rtha} + Tamil suffix \emph{-m}.
\end{itemize}

This intra-word biscriptal coupling violates the foundational assumption of nearly all modern OCR architectures: that a whitespace-delimited word---and typically an entire text line---belongs to a single writing system with uniform baseline geometry and a single character transition distribution. At an intra-word \texttt{dev} $\to$ \texttt{tam} junction, the printing type for the final Devan\={a}gar\={\i} \emph{ak\d{s}ara} frequently extends its upper horizontal headline (\emph{Shiro-rekh\={a}}) $1\text{--}3\text{ px}$ into the bounding column of the first Tamil glyph, creating a physical ink bridge between the two scripts.

\subsection{The Granularity--Context Paradox}

When digitizing a monumental biscriptal corpus such as the four-volume \emph{Vy\={a}khy\={a}na-pa\~{n}caka-sahita \'{S}r\={\i}mad-Rahasya-Traya-S\={a}ra} (2,970 PDF pages) and the two-volume \emph{S\={a}rabodhin\={\i}} edition (1,246 PDF pages), a fundamental tension emerges between \textbf{script homogeneity} (which requires fine-grained spatial segmentation down to sub-word fragments) and \textbf{visual/linguistic context} (which modern sequence-to-sequence and cloud document OCR engines require for accurate recognition). We call this the \textbf{Granularity--Context Paradox}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{At Coarse Granularity (Full Page or Full Line):} Visual and linguistic context is maximal, but script homogeneity is violated. Commercial VLM/cloud OCR engines (such as Google Drive Document OCR) and scene-text engines (PaddleOCR) lock onto the majority script of the line and force the minority script through the wrong decoder---transliterating Devan\={a}gar\={\i} into Tamil phonetics or garbled Latin (\texttt{tafuqGiiH}), or scrambling dense multi-commentary blocks (\textbf{0.435--0.710 CER}).
    \item \textbf{At Fine Granularity (Isolated Word or Sub-Word Fragment):} Script homogeneity is achieved by splitting mixed words at script boundaries, but visual and linguistic context collapses. When isolated 1-word or sub-word crops are arranged on synthetic ``faux pages'' for cloud OCR, the recognizer loses its multi-word horizontal baseline estimate and language-model priors. Consequently, Google Drive OCR drops \textbf{76\%--100\% of short Devan\={a}gar\={\i} stems} and misreads short Tamil suffixes as ASCII/Latin lookalikes (\texttt{LD IT 60T,}, \texttt{6T601M,}) because the 4-character ASCII row tag (\texttt{\#T01}) dominates the line-level script detector.
    \item \textbf{With Custom Dual-Script Models Trained from Scratch:} Training a unified CTC recognizer over $\Sigma_{\text{dev}} \cup \Sigma_{\text{tam}}$ encounters severe \textbf{capacity and lexicon starvation}: even with \textbf{92,976 verified real word crops}, a 4.1M-parameter Kraken VGSL word model tops out at \textbf{90.34\% character accuracy} (\textbf{80.53\% word accuracy}), translating to $\sim 100,000$ character errors per volume and \textbf{1,456 impossible intra-word script chimeras}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Weakly Supervised Dataset Construction via Render-and-DP Alignment}

To train and evaluate real-scan script classifiers, boundary detectors, and word recognizers without prohibitive manual bounding-box annotation, we developed a \textbf{Render-and-DP Weakly Supervised Alignment Pipeline} using two digitized volumes of the \emph{S\={a}rabodhin\={\i}} edition (\texttt{RTS\_sb\_01}, 630 pages; \texttt{RTS\_sb\_02}, 616 pages).

For each scanned page $p$, we partition the page at the horizontal separator rule into the upper \texttt{core} (\emph{m\={u}lam}) block and lower \texttt{comm} (\emph{S\={a}rabodhin\={\i}}) block. Candidate word bounding boxes $B = (b_1, \dots, b_M)$ from \texttt{tesseract -l tam+san --psm 6 tsv} are aligned against ground-truth tokens $W = (w_1, \dots, w_N)$ via monotonic dynamic programming that combines string similarity with direct visual template similarity (rendering each ground-truth token $w_j$ into a grayscale bitmap $\tilde{I}(w_j)$ using \emph{Lohit-Devanagari} and \emph{Noto Sans Tamil} fonts). Retaining pairs with confidence $\ge 0.45$ yields \textbf{95,411 high-confidence real word-image mappings} across \textbf{2,203 block JSON files}: \textbf{36,322 (38.1\%)} pure Devan\={a}gar\={\i}, \textbf{44,881 (47.0\%)} pure Tamil, and \textbf{14,208 (14.9\%)} intra-word mixed tokens.

\section{Methodology: Phrase-Strip Script Separation and Context-Engineered Faux Pages}

Our production pipeline (\texttt{tadevaOCR}) consists of eight stages:

\subsection{Stage 1: Pitch-Based Recursive Line Segmentation}
Grayscale pages (300 DPI) are binarized via an adaptive percentile threshold $T = \min(200, \max(150, P_{12}(G) + 25))$, and full-width horizontal separator rules ($\text{row\_ink} > 0.55 W_{\text{span}}$) are zeroed out. Using robust estimates of single-line height $H_{\text{unit}} = P_{35}(\{h_i\})$ and inter-line pitch $P_{\text{line}} = \text{median}(\{\Delta y_i\})$, any vertical band exceeding
\begin{equation}
H_{\max} = \max\left(H_{\text{unit}} + 0.55 P_{\text{line}},\; 1.55 H_{\text{unit}}\right)
\end{equation}
is recursively subdivided at local minima of a 5-pixel box-filtered projection profile within $\pm 0.35 P_{\text{line}}$ of expected pitch multiples, resolving multi-line merges caused by touching Tamil descenders and Devan\={a}gar\={\i} \emph{m\={a}tr\={a}s}.

\subsection{Stage 2: Mid-Band Ink-Filtered Word Segmentation}
Candidate word spans $[x_0, x_1]$ separated by vertical valleys $\ge 4\text{ px}$ must satisfy a dual ink-density invariant:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{y=0}^{H-1} \sum_{x=x_0}^{x_1-1} \mathbb{I}[\text{dark}(y, x)] \ge 18 \;\;\land\;\; \sum_{y=\lfloor 0.25H \rfloor}^{\lceil 0.75H \rceil} \sum_{x=x_0}^{x_1-1} \mathbb{I}[\text{dark}(y, x)] \ge 8,
\end{equation}
rejecting inter-line ascender/descender bleed.

\subsection{Stage 3: Sliding 48$\times$48 Patch CNN and Minimum-Ink Splitting}
A 4-class (\texttt{dev}, \texttt{tam}, \texttt{other}, \texttt{null}) $48\times 48$ residual CNN (\texttt{script\_cls.pt}, 1.2M parameters) retrained on 69,366 real word crops (\textbf{0.9921 validation accuracy}) slides across each word at \texttt{STRIDE = 8 px}, using true midpoint sampling $\lfloor(x_0+x_1)/2\rfloor$ when $w \le 40\text{ px}$. After folding short/interior \texttt{other} runs narrower than $\max(20\text{ px}, \lfloor 0.45h \rfloor)$, any intra-word \texttt{dev} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{tam} transition at $\hat{x}$ is snapped via \texttt{\_safe\_split\_x} across $[\hat{x}-14, \hat{x}+14]$:
\begin{equation}
x^* = \begin{cases}
\arg\min_{x,\, \text{col}(x)=0} |x - \hat{x}| & \text{if } \exists x: \text{col}(x)=0, \\
\arg\min_{x} \left(\text{col}(x) + 0.15|x - \hat{x}|\right) & \text{if } \min_x \text{col}(x) \le 6, \\
\hat{x} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Allowing $\min_x \text{col}(x) \le 6$ cleanly severs $1\text{--}3\text{ px}$ \emph{Shiro-rekh\={a}} headline overhangs without truncating either glyph.

\subsection{Stages 4--5: \texttt{pre\_space} Tracking, Punctuation Absorption, and Phrase-Strip Coalescing}
Sub-word splits receive $\text{pre\_space} = \text{False}$, as do valley-split pairs where $\text{prev\_script} = \text{dev}$, $\text{curr\_script} = \text{tam}$, and $\text{gap} \le 5\text{ px}$. Whole-word \texttt{other} tokens (dandas, numerals, hyphens, low-ink punctuation) are absorbed into the adjacent \texttt{dev} or \texttt{tam} run on the same line. Consecutive runs sharing the same script on a line are then coalesced into a \textbf{single multi-word phrase strip}, reducing segment count by $\sim 2.5\times$ and preserving horizontal baseline and language-model context. Internal whitespace spans $> 36\text{ px}$ are compacted to $22\text{ px}$.

\subsection{Stages 6--8: Context-Engineered Faux Pages, Phonotactic Script Anchors, and Collation}
Coalesced strips are packed onto separate \texttt{\_dev\_} and \texttt{\_tam\_} faux pages (40 rows/page, $\text{ROW\_PITCH} = 136\text{ px}$, strip height $\le 82\text{ px}$, vertical gap $54\text{ px}$). Row tags \texttt{\#T01..\#T40} end at $x \approx 118\text{ px}$ and strips begin at $x = 146\text{ px}$, yielding a \textbf{horizontal gap of $28\text{ px}$} ($1:2$ horizontal-to-vertical ratio), preventing vertical column detachment.

Crucially, on narrow rows ($w < 460\text{ px}$), the 4 ASCII characters \texttt{\#Txx} would otherwise outnumber a 3-letter Tamil suffix, flipping Google Drive's line-level script detector to Latin. We append a crisp \textbf{6-consonant phonotactic script anchor} $36\text{ px}$ to the right of every narrow crop (the first six standard stops in Tamil or Devan\={a}gar\={\i}), guaranteeing a $\ge 2.25\times$ Indic glyph majority and locking the recognizer into the target script. During collation, anchors are stripped, OCR-corrupted tags (\texttt{\#TOI}, \texttt{\#101}) are normalized, boundary-word duplicates are removed, and horizontal rules (\texttt{---}) are used to dissect pages into their 5 constituent commentaries.

Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} summarizes our controlled evaluation across 6 OCR paradigms on the \texttt{RTS\_sb\_01} camera-photographed blocks. Our \texttt{tadevaOCR} pipeline achieves \textbf{0.112 raw CER}, a \textbf{3.88$\times$ error reduction} over unsplit Google Drive OCR (\texttt{0.435}), \textbf{3.14$\times$} over bilingual Tesseract (\texttt{0.352}), and \textbf{2.13$\times$} over our custom dual-script Kraken line CTC model (\texttt{0.238}). Forensic inspection reveals that the \texttt{0.112} figure on \texttt{RTS\_sb\_01} is inflated by three corpus-specific factors: (1) quote-glyph differences (\texttt{core/p0043} drops from \texttt{0.033} to \textbf{0.0000 CER} after normalizing curly vs.\ straight quotes); (2) 3D page-curl camera warp in the lower third of outlier block \texttt{comm/p0043} (\texttt{0.218} CER, evaluated on Oct~21 prior to real-scan classifier retraining and script anchors); and (3) non-diplomatic ground-truth normalization in \texttt{RTS\_sb\_01/gt}, where printed Devan\={a}gar\={\i} \emph{anusv\={a}ras} (\texttt{-ं}) were transcribed as Tamil \texttt{-ம்}. When the finalized \texttt{tadevaOCR} pipeline is evaluated on flat 300-DPI scans of the five-commentary target edition against diplomatic ground truth (\texttt{p00029\_errors\_marked.md}, 35 lines, 2,374 non-space characters across \emph{m\={u}lam} and all 5 commentaries), it achieves a \textbf{true diplomatic CER of \texttt{0.0067} (0.67\% CER / 99.33\% character accuracy; 16 edits out of 2,374 characters)}.

Table~\ref{tab:boundary} demonstrates that structural geometry (\texttt{projection-glyph} DP at \textbf{0.0320 MAE} and \texttt{shiro-rekhA} at \textbf{0.0341 MAE}) and fixed-aspect-ratio $48\times 48$ sliding patches with minimum-ink snapping (\texttt{0.0412 MAE}) dramatically outperform aspect-ratio-warped coordinate regression CNNs (\texttt{0.1551 MAE}), while sliding patches further generalize to multi-transition words (\texttt{tam} $\to$ \texttt{dev} $\to$ \texttt{tam}).

In our Volume~1 ablation study (4 pages, 568 phrase strips), adding the $28\text{ px}$ tag-to-strip proximity invariant and the 6-consonant phonotactic script anchor raises first-pass \texttt{\#Txx} tag recovery to \textbf{568/568 (100.0\%)}, recovers \textbf{110 intra-word mixed tokens} (vs.\ \texttt{0} in unsplit Drive and \texttt{14} in wordwise Drive), and reduces stray ASCII/Latin characters from \texttt{74} to \texttt{4} (\textbf{94.6\% reduction}).

Finally, Table~\ref{tab:volumes} reports the full 4-volume production run across \textbf{2,970 PDF pages}, yielding \textbf{6,674,633 Unicode characters}, \textbf{97,198 lines}, and \textbf{57,150 intra-word mixed words}. Using the 8,747 detected horizontal section rules in Volumes~1--3, our structural dissector partitioned all \textbf{2,103 chapter pages} (\textbf{11,683 content blocks}: 1,691 \emph{m\={u}lam} blocks and 9,992 commentary blocks) into \textbf{165 per-commentary, per-adhik\={a}ra files} across the five commentaries (\emph{S\={a}rad\={\i}pik\={a}}, \emph{S\={a}r\={a}sv\={a}din\={\i}}, \emph{S\={a}raprak\={a}\'{s}ik\={a}}, \emph{S\={a}ravivara\d{n}\={\i}}, and \emph{S\={a}raprak\={a}\'{s}ik\={a}-sa\.{n}graha\d{h}}) with \textbf{zero unresolved blocks}.

\section{Conclusion}

By identifying and resolving the \textbf{Granularity--Context Paradox} through real-scan-retrained sliding-patch script separation, minimum-ink \emph{Shiro-rekh\={a}} splitting, same-script phrase-strip coalescing, and phonotactically anchored faux pages, we unlock high-capacity document OCR for complex intra-word biscriptal corpora without requiring costly from-scratch language model training.
